**Date:** August 2026

## 1. Executive Summary

**Taalas**, a ~25-person Toronto startup founded in 2023 by Tenstorrent founder Ljubisa Bajic, built the most extreme point on the AI-hardware specialization spectrum: chips that permanently hardwire a *single LLM's weights* into mask-ROM on a logic die, with no HBM and no external DRAM. Its first silicon, **HC1** (unveiled Feb 19, 2026), is an 815 mm², 53B-transistor TSMC N6 die that runs Llama 3.1 8B — and nothing else — at a claimed 16,960 tokens/sec *per user* on a ~200–250 W air-cooled PCIe card ([Next Platform](https://www.nextplatform.com/compute/2026/02/19/taalas-etches-ai-models-onto-transistors-to-rocket-boost-inference/4092140), [EE Times](https://www.eetimes.com/taalas-specializes-to-extremes-for-extraordinary-token-speed/)). The chip is real and publicly demoable; the headline number is vendor-reported, with the best independent measurement (~14,357 tok/s, Karl Freund) landing ~15% below it ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/karlfreund/2026/02/19/taalas-launches-hardcore-chip-with-insane-ai-inference-performance/)).

**AMD announced a definitive agreement to acquire Taalas on August 6, 2026** — terms undisclosed, closing expected Q4 2026, team joining AMD's AI Group under SVP Vamsi Boppana ([AMD press release](https://ir.amd.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1296/amd-acquires-taalas-to-advance-compute-solutions-for-rapidly-growing-ai-inference-market)). It is AMD's third AI acquisition in nine months and came two weeks after AMD's ultra-low-latency inference partnership with Cerebras ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/06/amd-buys-taalas-startup-that-hardwires-ai-models-into-its-silicon.html)).

The core findings of this report:

1. **The technical thesis is coherent and partially demonstrated.** Storing weights in mask-ROM collapses the memory wall for decode: per-user token rates ~50–70x what batched GPUs deliver, at an order of magnitude lower power, are consistent with the physics. What is *not* demonstrated: the two-metal-layer / two-month respin cadence (the entire answer to model churn — no second Taalas chip had publicly shipped as of Aug 2026), aggregate throughput and utilization economics (the design is effectively batch-≈1), and output quality versus the BF16 reference (no independent MMLU/GSM8K/perplexity eval exists anywhere in the public record).
2. **The industry pattern is absorption, not independent victory.** Every serious specialist has either been bought by an incumbent (Taalas→AMD; Untether's team→AMD; Groq's IP and team→Nvidia in a reported ~$20B December 2025 licensing deal — an extraordinary figure this corpus could not fully corroborate; see §3), coupled to a whale customer (Cerebras→OpenAI; d-Matrix→Microsoft orbit), or remains pre-revenue on billion-dollar marks (Etched, MatX, Fractile).
3. **The playbook is now legible.** Between the public architecture literature (§4) and the verified cost datapoints from Groq, Taalas, Etched, and the open-silicon ecosystem, one can lay out a staged, numbers-attached path from a $100 Tiny Tapeout tile to a $50–75M seed-tier LLM ASIC to a $500M+ HBM-class program (§5). The single most important design decision is where the weights live; everything else follows from that.
4. **Honest verdict (§6):** model-specific silicon wins when a weight-stable model family meets high-volume, power-capped, latency-priced demand for ≥12 months; it loses to GPU fungibility and ~10x/year software-side $/token deflation everywhere else. The AMD acquisition is a portfolio bet — an option on the "stable-model, extreme-economics" tier of inference — not a declaration that GPUs are finished.

## 2. Taalas

- **Founders:** Ljubisa Bajic (CEO), Drago Ignjatovic, Lejla Bajic — all ex-Tenstorrent, with deep prior AMD lineage (Bajic: AMD architect/director of IC design, one-year Nvidia stint; Ignjatovic: AMD senior design engineer who took over Bajic's AMD role before joining Tenstorrent as VP of hardware) ([Next Platform](https://www.nextplatform.com/compute/2026/02/19/taalas-etches-ai-models-onto-transistors-to-rocket-boost-inference/4092140)). This deliberately inverts Tenstorrent's programmable-dataflow thesis: keep the graph-to-silicon compilation idea, freeze one graph per chip.

### 2.2 The technical thesis, as precisely as public sources allow

**The mechanism.** The die splits into two regions ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/systems/2026/08/06/amd-acquires-ai-chip-startup-taalas-to-boost-inference-performance-by-etching-models-into-silicon/5284344)):

1. **A mask-ROM "recall fabric"** holding the frozen base-model weights. Per Bajic to The Next Platform: "we can store four bits away and do the multiply related to it — everything — with a single transistor." The patent family (notably [US20250123802A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2025217724A1/en), "Large parameter set computation accelerator," plus WO2025217724A1 "Mask Programmable ROM using Shared Connections") describes the trick: multiplication is eliminated by a **Multiply-Select-Add** scheme in which shared hardware continuously pre-computes all 16 possible products of an incoming activation against every possible 4-bit weight value, and each weight's ROM transistor merely *routes* the correct pre-computed "product line" into an adder tree — the metal mask, set at manufacture, is the weight ([yongwei.site analysis](https://yongwei.site/en/taalas-hc1-arch/)). Storage and arithmetic collapse into the same transistor; density approaches DRAM-class in a logic process, with zero weight-movement energy.
2. **A programmable SRAM "recall fabric"** holding everything input-dependent that *cannot* be hardwired: the **KV cache** (attention keys/values grow with the conversation and are unknowable at mask time), plus **LoRA/fine-tuned weight overlays**. Context window length is configurable, bounded by SRAM capacity — which Taalas has never disclosed ([EE Times](https://www.eetimes.com/taalas-specializes-to-extremes-for-extraordinary-token-speed/)).

**Reconciling the weight-format reports.** The corpus contains an apparent contradiction (4-bit per transistor vs "3-bit/6-bit mixed"), which resolves cleanly from Taalas's own blog: the patent describes the *general* 16-product-line 4-bit mechanism; **HC1's shipped instantiation uses a custom 3-bit base data type with a mixture of 3-bit and 6-bit parameters**, because "low-precision parameter formats were not standardized" when design began — and Taalas concedes this "introduces some quality degradations relative to GPU benchmarks." **HC2, the second generation, moves to standard 4-bit floating point (MXFP4), "addressing these limitations"** ([taalas.com, "The Path to Ubiquitous AI"](https://taalas.com/the-path-to-ubiquitous-ai/)). That format migration is itself a revealed-preference datapoint: Taalas judged its own 3-bit floor too aggressive.

**The respin story — a claim, not a demonstrated capability.** Taalas's answer to model obsolescence: only ~2 of the chip's 100+ mask layers (top metal) are model-specific, covering *both* weights and dataflow; pre-built base wafers plus an in-house model-to-layout compiler flow (design generation claimed at ~1 week) yield a new hardwired chip roughly **two months** after a new model drops, via a process "worked out with TSMC" ([EE Times](https://www.eetimes.com/taalas-specializes-to-extremes-for-extraordinary-token-speed/)). This borrows directly from early-2000s structured-ASIC practice. **It has never been publicly demonstrated:** as of August 2026 no second Taalas chip had shipped, and the claim that two metal layers suffice for *architecturally* different models (different layer counts, GQA configs, MoE routing) is strong and untested. Note also the schedule history: the March 2024 plan promised Q3 2024 tape-out and Q1 2025 customer availability; first public silicon arrived February 2026 — a slip of roughly 1.5 years ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/ai-startup-taalas-comes-out-of-stealth-raises-50m-for-llm-chips/)).

**HC1 verified vs claimed:**

**What the vendor numbers quietly assume: batch ≈ 1.** HC1 "doesn't need to batch queries" ([Next Platform](https://www.nextplatform.com/compute/2026/02/19/taalas-etches-ai-models-onto-transistors-to-rocket-boost-inference/4092140)) — weight-fetch cost is zero, so batching buys nothing. But SRAM still bounds how many sessions' KV state fit. Independent probing of the public endpoint found an input cap of ~6,064 prefill tokens (~6.9K effective context vs Llama 3.1's native 128K) and a modest endpoint concurrency ceiling ([chatjimmy-api probe](https://github.com/0xMH/chatjimmy-api), [llm-boundary-tests](https://github.com/KTS-o7/llm-boundary-tests)). KV arithmetic explains why: Llama 3.1 8B GQA needs ~64 KB/token at 8-bit KV, so a full ~7K-token session is ~220–440 MB — against a plausible ~400–600 MB of total die SRAM. That implies roughly **1–3 full-context concurrent sessions per chip** (derived estimate, not a disclosure), so aggregate per-chip throughput is on the order of the per-user figure, not GPU-style batch-256 amortization. On *aggregate* tokens/sec/chip, HC1 and a well-batched GPU are far closer than the per-user headline suggests. The honest framing: **Taalas sells per-user speed, energy, and system cost — not necessarily high-batch $/token** — and every published $/token comparison mixes latency-optimized per-user figures against competitors' batch economics without normalization.

**Quality: publicly unmeasured.** No independent MMLU/GSM8K/perplexity comparison of the hardwired 3/6-bit model against BF16 Llama 3.1 8B exists as of Aug 2026. The only quantified third-party datapoint is a small LangWatch codegen benchmark (ChatJimmy 0.69 vs 0.91–0.93 for frontier small models — but with no BF16 same-model arm, it doesn't isolate quantization loss); the only same-model A/B is a single HN commenter's spot check finding "nearly identical" outputs vs ollama. The public endpoint returns `logprobs: null`, precluding perplexity evals; the official API (which exposes logprobs) closed to new applicants Aug 2, 2026. So all $/token claims compare quantized tokens to full-precision tokens with zero published quality normalization.

**Licensing (a question no coverage has raised):** The Llama 3.1 Community License facially permits selling silicon "containing" the weights, subject to shipping the Agreement, "Built with Llama" notices, and AUP flow-down; the 700M-MAU clause was irrelevant to startup-sized Taalas but is a genuinely open question for AMD, and the license's "non-transferable" grant and delete-on-termination remedy are unlitigated edge cases for irrevocable etched inventory ([license text](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/meta-llama/llama-models/main/models/llama3_1/LICENSE)). MIT-licensed models (DeepSeek-R1 line) are the legally cleanest hardwiring candidates. **No public evidence exists of any bespoke Meta rights deal by Taalas or AMD.**

### 2.3 The AMD acquisition — facts and rationale

**Facts:** Definitive agreement announced August 6, 2026; **terms undisclosed** (no outlet reported even a rumored price); expected close Q4 2026 pending regulatory approval; a real acquisition, not an acquihire; team joins AMD's AI Group under Vamsi Boppana; AMD plans to integrate the technology into its accelerator roadmap "alongside AMD Instinct GPUs" ([AMD IR](https://ir.amd.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1296/amd-acquires-taalas-to-advance-compute-solutions-for-rapidly-growing-ai-inference-market), [EE Times](https://www.eetimes.com/ai-chip-startup-taalas-acquired-by-amd/)).

**Why AMD bought it** (evidence-based reading):

1. **Portfolio strategy, stated explicitly.** Boppana: "the right compute solutions for every AI workload." Lisa Su at Advancing AI (July 23, 2026): "there's no one-size-fits-all as it comes to chips." Taalas is the extreme-specialization endpoint of a spectrum AMD now spans: Instinct MI400/Helios racks → Cerebras partnership (low-latency wafer-scale decode) → Taalas (hardwired models) ([wccftech](https://wccftech.com/amd-snaps-up-taalas-weeks-after-cerebras-deal-chasing-chips-that-bake-ai-models-into-silicon/)).
2. **A systematic inference-stack roll-up.** Taalas is the eighth piece: Silo AI ($665M, models), ZT Systems ($4.9B, racks), Enosemi (optics), Brium (compilers), Untether AI's team (at-memory inference), MK1 (inference software), MEXT (memory tiering), Taalas (model-specific silicon) — every layer of inference cost structure ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/amd-extends-acquisition-run-brings-software-optimization-firm-brium-and-staff-from-recently-shuttered-untether-ai-into-the-fold/)).
3. **The memory wall and HBM scarcity.** Weights-in-ROM eliminates the single most constrained, most expensive line in inference BOMs (HBM3E at $13–17/GB, allocation-locked through 2026) ([TrendForce](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2025/12/24/news-samsung-sk-hynix-reportedly-plan-20-hbm3e-price-hike-for-2026-as-nvidia-h200-asic-demand-rises/)).
4. **The disaggregation fit.** Analysts (Austin Lyons) note the natural system play: Helios GPUs for prefill, Taalas silicon for decode — at the cost of pinning a deployment to one model ([Chipstrat](https://www.chipstrat.com/p/amd-bought-a-chip-that-runs-one-model)).
5. **Keeping it out of other hands, cheaply.** Two weeks after strong Q2 earnings (record $11.5B revenue, DC +107%) in a market where Nvidia had just paid a reported fortune for Groq's inference IP, an undisclosed-price purchase of 24 ex-AMD engineers and a proven no-HBM inference technology is a low-cost option ([AMD Q2 2026](https://ir.amd.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1295/amd-reports-second-quarter-2026-financial-results)).
6. **Homecoming.** Bajic and Ignjatovic are former AMD architects; AMD emphasized retaining Canadian talent.

**Roadmap status at acquisition — slipping, and inconsistently reported.** Corpus sources give conflicting HC2 dates (a second HC1-platform model "spring/Q2 2026"; HC2 silicon "summer 2026" per The Register vs "winter 2026" per Taalas's blog; frontier-class by "winter 2026"). As of August 2026 **no second product is confirmed shipped**, and given the original ~1.5-year slip, the roadmap should be read as *slipping*, not as two valid snapshots. The DeepSeek R1 671B figures (~30 chips, ~12,000 tok/s/user, ~7.6¢/M tokens) are **explicitly simulation**, and note the two circulating $/token figures are for different systems: $0.0075/M is the single-chip 8B vendor claim; $0.076/M is the simulated 30-chip 671B pipeline.

## 3. The Landscape

### 3.1 AMD's inference strategy in one paragraph

AMD's 2026 position: Instinct MI355X shipping (288GB HBM3E, inference-first), MI400/Helios racks entering shipment H2 2026 (~$5.25M/rack, 31TB HBM4), anchored by 6GW OpenAI (with a 160M-share $0.01 warrant), a reported ~$60B/6GW Meta deal (deal value conflicts across sources, $60B vs $100B; the claim that Meta received its *own* identical 160M-share penny warrant is **unverified against SEC filings** and should be treated as reported-only), 2GW Anthropic plus up to $5B equity, and expanded Azure Helios ([AMD/OpenAI](https://ir.amd.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1260/amd-and-openai-announce-strategic-partnership-to-deploy-6-gigawatts-of-amd-gpus), [AMD/Anthropic](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/07/22/3331418/0/en/amd-and-anthropic-announce-strategic-partnership-to-deploy-up-to-2-gigawatts-of-amd-instinct-mi450-series-gpus.html)). On top of that GPU base, AMD is explicitly building a heterogeneous inference tier: Cerebras for ultra-low-latency decode ([AMD/Cerebras](https://ir.amd.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1293/amd-and-cerebras-announce-industry-leading-ultra-low-latency-and-high-throughput-ai-inference-solution)), Taalas for hardwired-model economics, XDNA NPUs and Versal for edge — against Nvidia's single-architecture stance.

### 3.3 What the precedents teach

- **Groq** (ISCA 2020/2022 papers; deterministic SRAM-only TSP): proved per-user speed sells, but 230MB SRAM/chip meant 576 chips for Llama-2 70B — capacity scaling is the SRAM school's structural weakness ([SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/groq-inference-tokenomics-speed-but)). Its endgame: the corpus reports Nvidia paid **~$20B in Dec 2025 for a *non-exclusive* license plus ~90% of the engineering team** ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/12/24/nvidia-buying-ai-chip-startup-groq-for-about-20-billion-biggest-deal.html), [Groq newsroom](https://groq.com/newsroom/groq-and-nvidia-enter-non-exclusive-inference-technology-licensing-agreement-to-accelerate-ai-inference-at-global-scale)). **Caution:** this figure is extraordinary (~3x Nvidia's largest prior acquisition, for a non-exclusive license), sits unreconciled with Jensen Huang's contemporaneous public dismissal of ASICs, and could not be independently re-verified within this corpus; treat the magnitude as reported, the deal's existence and licensing-plus-acquihire structure as better attested. Either way, the *lesson* stands: specialist inference IP monetized via incumbent absorption, not market victory.
- **Cerebras** (wafer-scale, 44GB on-chip SRAM at ~21 PB/s): survived a near-fatal G42 revenue-concentration crisis, then coupled to OpenAI (750MW through 2028, reported at >$10B by [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/14/cerebras-scores-openai-deal-worth-over-10-billion.html), with later coverage citing >$20B — unresolved) and IPO'd in May 2026. **The corpus's IPO figures are internally contradictory** ($185/share and $5.5B raised with a ~$48B first-day cap, vs a "~$26B" summary figure; a $5.5B raise would rank among the largest tech IPOs ever) — the safe statement is: Cerebras completed a strongly received IPO in May 2026 after re-filing; exact proceeds/valuation need primary sourcing ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/14/cerebras-raises-5-5b-kicking-off-2026s-ipo-season-with-a-bang/)). Lesson: a decade, a whale, and a crisis stand between wafer-scale silicon and a public exit.
- **Etched** (transformer-only Sohu): first-pass A0 silicon on N4P shown June 2026 after ~$800M raised and a ~2-year slip; $10.3B valuation in July 2026 with **zero independent benchmarks and nothing purchasable** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/23/ai-chip-startup-etched-defies-skeptics-hits-10-3b-valuation-from-big-name-investors/)). A widely repeated claim that MoE models (DeepSeek V4, Qwen3-235B) "broke" the transformers-forever premise **overreaches**: MoE models *are* transformers; any Sohu incompatibility (analyst-reported, contested, not confirmed by Etched) is a memory-capacity/routing limitation, not architecture churn — the real churn risks are hybrid SSM/Mamba layers and attention variants ([Spheron analysis](https://www.spheron.network/blog/etched-ai-sohu-vs-nvidia-transformer-asic-inference/), [wafer.substack critique](https://wafer.substack.com/p/breaking-down-etcheds-sohu)).
- **The graveyard and near-graveyard:** Untether AI shut down mid-2025, team acqui-hired by AMD for reportedly <$100M, customers stranded ([EE Times](https://www.eetimes.com/untether-ai-shuts-down-engineering-team-joins-amd/)); Graphcore sold to SoftBank ~$500–600M, below capital raised; Mythic (analog) nearly died in 2022 before a 2025 re-raise; SambaNova whipsawed from collapsed ~$1.6B Intel talks to a $1B raise at $11B on an inference refocus ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/08/sambanova-draws-1b-at-11b-valuation-in-series-f-first-close/)); memory-vendor PIM remains in standardization purgatory after five years of demos.
- **Bitcoin's CPU→GPU→FPGA→ASIC arc** (~250,000x efficiency gain) is the maximal specialization precedent — but its enabling condition was that **SHA-256 is frozen by protocol forever**. LLMs are "transformer-shaped but internally churning" ([Bitdeer history](https://www.bitdeer.com/news/bitcoin-mining-efficiency-the-road-from-5000000-to-5-jth-and-beyond)).
- **Video codecs** are the strongest *positive* precedent: once H.264 froze (2003), fixed-function blocks with ~20x power advantages became universal — but as *hardened kernels inside general chips*, not codec-only computers ([arXiv 2402.09001](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.09001)). That maps to Nvidia's transformer-engine strategy more than to Etched.
- **eASIC structured ASICs** — the closest historical analog to Taalas's two-metal-layer flow — survived 19 years as a niche before absorption by Intel (2018). The intermediate specialization point tends to be a real but small market whose players end up inside platform owners ([Intel](https://www.intc.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/143/intel-editorial-intel-to-acquire-easic-adding-to-its)) — exactly the pattern now repeating.

## 4. The Architecture Literature Map

The field's center of gravity shifted ~2023 from dataflow innovation to memory-system innovation, because autoregressive decode is a GEMV workload at ~1 FLOP/byte at low batch — roughly 300x below a modern GPU's roofline ridge. Organized by design-space axis (venue, year, takeaway):

**Foundations / industrial doctrine**
- **TPU v1** — Jouppi et al., ISCA 2017 ([arXiv 1704.04760](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04760)). 65,536-MAC INT8 systolic array; roofline analysis showed production inference was bandwidth-bound even then. *A huge MAC array is wasted without matching bandwidth.*
- **"Ten Lessons" / TPUv4i** — ISCA 2021 ([DOI](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1109/ISCA52012.2021.00010)). The key industrial inference-design paper: compiler (not binary) compatibility; optimize TCO not unit cost; DNNs grow ~1.5x/yr so leave headroom; some inference needs FP; latency, not batch, is the app constraint. *The standing argument against over-specialization.*
- **TPU v4** — ISCA 2023 ([arXiv 2304.01433](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01433)): optical circuit switching as an architectural axis. **TPUv7 Ironwood** — Hot Chips 2025 ([slides](https://hc2025.hotchips.org/assets/program/conference/day2/61_Google_Ironwood-Final.pdf)): first TPU designed *for* LLM inference (4,614 FP8 TFLOPS, 192GB HBM3E, 9,216-chip pods).

**Dataflow**
- **Eyeriss** — ISCA 2016 ([DOI](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3007787.3001177)) + Sze/Chen/Emer tutorial (Proc. IEEE 2017): the weight-/output-/row-stationary taxonomy; data movement, not arithmetic, dominates energy (DRAM access ≈ 100–200x a MAC). *For decode GEMV, weight reuse ≈ 1, so classic dataflow optimization yields little — hence the pivot to memory.*
- **EIE** — Han et al., ISCA 2016 ([arXiv 1602.01528](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.01528)): compressed model entirely in SRAM; SRAM ~120x cheaper per access than DRAM. Ancestor of the "never touch DRAM for weights" school.

**Memory: weights-in-SRAM school**
- **Microsoft Brainwave** — ISCA 2018 ([MSR](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/a-configurable-cloud-scale-dnn-processing-unit/)): *the* published precedent for model-pinned weights in on-chip memory at datacenter scale, batch-1, with narrow block floating point (ms-fp8/9) to stretch SRAM. Production-deployed in Bing/Azure. Direct ancestor of both Taalas and MX formats.
- **Groq TSP** — ISCA 2020 + ISCA 2022 ([paper](https://groq.com/isca-2022-paper/)): fully deterministic, compiler-scheduled, 220MB SRAM, no DRAM; ISCA 2022 extends determinism across racks via deskew hardware.
- **Cerebras deep dive** — Sean Lie, IEEE Micro 2023; independent comparison [arXiv 2503.11698](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.11698). Wafer-scale converts the memory wall into an on-wafer NoC/partitioning problem.
- **WaferLLM** — OSDI 2025 ([USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi25/presentation/he)): first systems paper actually running LLMs on wafer-scale; GEMV 606x faster than A100, but only with entirely new distributed MeshGEMV algorithms (PLMR model). *SRAM machines need new algorithms, not just ports.*
- **Chiplet Cloud** — [arXiv 2307.02666](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.02666): the most explicit published TCO-per-token methodology for weights-in-SRAM ASIC economics (small dies for yield, per-model provisioning). Paper design only.

**Memory: PIM / near-data school**
- **AttAcc / NeuPIMs / IANUS** — all ASPLOS 2024 ([IANUS DOI](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3620666.3651324), [NeuPIMs arXiv 2403.00579](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.00579)): the now-dominant heterogeneous template — NPU for compute-bound prefill/FFN, compute-in-DRAM for decode attention GEMV. *Rationale: batching restores FFN intensity but attention stays per-request GEMV over a private KV cache — attention is the piece worth moving into memory.*
- **Duplex** — MICRO 2024 ([arXiv 2409.01141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01141)): per-layer Op/B becomes the runtime dispatch criterion (the roofline as scheduler); handles MoE/GQA.
- **Cambricon-LLM** — MICRO 2024 ([arXiv 2409.15654](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15654)): NPU + in-flash-compute chiplet; 70B on-device. The capacity tier below DRAM.
- **Real PIM silicon:** Samsung Aquabolt-XL HBM-PIM (Hot Chips 33/ISSCC 2021 — [slides](https://www.hc33.hotchips.org/assets/program/conference/day1/20210813_HC33_Aquabolt-XL_PIM_Jin_Kim_slide.pdf)), SK hynix AiM (ISSCC 2022) — fabricated, but ecosystem-blocked.
- **SpecPIM** (ASPLOS 2024) and **PAPI** (ASPLOS 2025, [DOI](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3676641.3716009)): speculative decoding and dynamic parallelism co-designed into PIM.

**Attention & KV handling**
- **A3** (HPCA 2020), **SpAtten** (HPCA 2021, [arXiv 2012.09852](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.09852)), **ELSA** (ISCA 2021): the attention-accelerator trilogy — attention units earn their area only via dynamic sparsity/approximation; dense decode attention is pure bandwidth.
- **PagedAttention/vLLM** (SOSP 2023) sets the software baseline; hardware follow-ons: **LongSight** (MICRO 2025, [DOI](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3725843.3756062)) for million-token sparse attention in compute-enabled memory; HiKV, AMMA, Sangam (2026 preprints) — KV is getting its own memory tier and management hardware.

**Phase disaggregation**
- **Splitwise** — ISCA 2024; **DistServe** — OSDI 2024 ([USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi24/presentation/zhong-yinmin)): prefill (100s of FLOP/byte, compute-bound) and decode (~1 FLOP/byte) on separately provisioned fleets; 7.4x more requests at same SLO. *Implication: future silicon can specialize per phase — different compute:bandwidth ratios for a prefill chip vs a decode chip. This is the intellectual basis of AMD's Helios+Cerebras and prospective Helios+Taalas pairings.*

**Precision**
- **Shared microexponents** — Microsoft, ISCA 2023 ([DOI](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3579371.3589351)) → **OCP Microscaling MX v1.0** ([spec](https://www.opencompute.org/documents/ocp-microscaling-formats-mx-v1-0-spec-final-pdf), with [arXiv 2310.10537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10537)): MXFP8/6/4, 32-element blocks with E8M0 scales; now native in Nvidia Blackwell — and adopted by Taalas HC2. *Format choice is a standards decision, not just an accuracy one.*
- **FP8 formats** — NVIDIA/Arm/Intel ([arXiv 2209.05433](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.05433)): E4M3/E5M2 baseline.
- **BitNet b1.58** — [arXiv 2402.17764](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17764): ternary weights make matmul multiplication-free; hardware follow-ons include LUT-based accelerators and **BitROM** compute-in-ROM for billion-parameter 1.58-bit inference ([arXiv 2509.08542](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.08542)) — the academic parallel to Taalas. Unproven at frontier scale.

**Mid-spectrum industrial designs & FPGA proving grounds**
- **FuriosaAI TCP** — ISCA 2024 industrial track ([slides](https://www.iscaconf.org/isca2024/slides/Session%207%20-%20TCP.pdf)): native tensor-contraction execution, shipped silicon. **HyperAccel LPU** — IEEE Micro 2024 ([arXiv 2408.07326](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07326)): compute matched exactly to memory bandwidth for latency-optimal decode.
- **DFX** (Hot Chips 2022) and **FlightLLM** — FPGA 2024 ([DOI](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3626202.3637562)): decode-stage multi-FPGA appliance with custom ISA; FPGA beating an A100 with sparse DSP chains. *FPGAs are where decode dataflows get de-risked before ASIC commitment.*

**Surveys (start here)**
- **LLM Inference Unveiled** — [arXiv 2402.16363](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16363): roofline analysis as the organizing framework (+ LLM-Viewer tool).
- **LLM Inference Acceleration: A Comprehensive Hardware Perspective** — [arXiv 2410.04466](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04466): broadest CPU/GPU/FPGA/ASIC/PIM taxonomy.
- **Kachris surveys** — [arXiv 2401.09890](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.09890), [2409.03384](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.03384): accelerator comparisons normalized to a common process node.

## 5. The Playbook: Designing an LLM-Specific ASIC

### Stage 0 — Spec: decide what you are actually building

Every downstream decision follows from five numbers you must fix first:

1. **Target model(s):** one frozen model (Taalas-class), a weight-swappable architecture class (Etched-class), or a phase (decode-only)? Check the license now, not later: MIT/Apache models (DeepSeek-R1, Qwen, Muse) are clean; Llama's community license permits embodiment but imposes notice/AUP/MAU machinery (§2.2).
2. **Batch and concurrency:** are you selling per-user tok/s (batch≈1, latency-priced — agents, real-time reasoning, voice) or aggregate $/token (batch 64–256)? These are *different chips*. The entire Taalas/Groq/Cerebras class is the former; do not benchmark yourself against GPU batch economics if you build it.
3. **Prefill vs decode:** decode is ~1 FLOP/byte GEMV; prefill is 100s of FLOP/byte GEMM ([DistServe](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi24/presentation/zhong-yinmin)). A decode-only chip paired with GPU prefill (Splitwise-style) is a legitimate, smaller-scope product — but the KV-cache handoff between the two becomes your hardest interface.
4. **Context length:** this is a *capacity* commitment. KV bytes/token = 2 × layers × kv_heads × head_dim × bytes. For Llama 3.1 8B (32L × 8KV × 128d): **64 KB/token at 8-bit**. 8K context = 512 MB/session; 128K = 8 GB/session. On-die SRAM designs cap context hard (HC1's public demo: ~7K); HBM designs don't. Decide which market you're in.
5. **Quality floor:** the demonstrated floor for weight-frozen silicon is mixed 3/6-bit with vendor-acknowledged (never quantified) quality loss; Taalas itself retreated to standard MXFP4 for gen 2. Plan a rigorous quantized-vs-BF16 eval (MMLU, GSM8K, perplexity) as a *release gate* — the absence of exactly this eval is the biggest credibility hole in every shipped specialist's story.

### Stage 1 — Memory strategy: the central decision

Work the arithmetic for your target model before drawing any block diagram. Example: **Llama-3.1-8B-class, batch 1 decode** — every generated token reads all weights once.

| Weight store | Weights (8B params) | Bandwidth available | Decode ceiling (tok/s/user) | Context capacity | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **On-die ROM/SRAM** (Taalas) | 4-bit ⇒ ~4 GB… fits only with ~3-bit + 815mm² N6 | effectively unlimited (weights don't move; MSA LUT scheme) | ~15–20K demonstrated | ~7K tokens (SRAM-bound) | No HBM BOM; reticle-limit die; yield/redundancy story undisclosed; model frozen |
| **On-die SRAM, multi-chip** (Groq/Cerebras) | 230MB–44GB per chip/wafer | ~80 TB/s per die – 21 PB/s per wafer | 1000s | SRAM-bound; weights crowd out KV | 70B needs 100s of chips or a wafer; interconnect determinism is the hard part |
| **HBM3E** (GPU/Etched class) | 4 GB (INT4) – 16 GB (FP16), trivial | ~4.8–8 TB/s | 4 GB/token ÷ 4.8 TB/s ≈ **~1,200** (INT4, theoretical; real ~50–70% MBU) | 100K+ easily | $13–17/GB, allocation-locked, CoWoS 40–52-wk lead times — **effectively closed to seed-stage teams** |
| **LPDDR5X/GDDR6** (Tenstorrent-style) | trivial | ~0.5–1 TB/s | ~125–250 | large | Cheap, procurable; per-user speed unremarkable; wins on cost/W for batch serving |
| **Flash/CXL tier** (Cambricon-LLM) | any size | low | single-digit | large | On-device / cold-expert tier only |

The lesson of the table: **per-user decode speed is purchased almost entirely with weight-store bandwidth**, and the only ways to beat HBM by 10x are to put weights on-die (SRAM/ROM) or into the memory itself (PIM). Seed-stage procurement reality makes the choice for you: you cannot get HBM allocation, so a lean team builds SRAM/ROM-resident (Groq 14nm, Taalas N6 precedents) or LPDDR/GDDR designs.

**KV cache is the part you cannot freeze.** Whatever you do with weights, provision programmable memory for KV + LoRA overlays (Taalas's SRAM fabric; the ASPLOS 2024 PIM papers' entire reason for existing). For long context, either attach DRAM for KV only, or adopt hardware KV sparsity/compression (SpAtten lineage, LongSight).

### Stage 2 — Architecture decisions

- **Compute style:** for decode, weight-stationary streaming (Brainwave/Groq/LPU) or in-memory selection (Taalas MSA, BitROM). A conventional MAC array sized for prefill will idle during decode — consider disaggregating phases instead of compromising one die.
- **Precision datapath:** implement MXFP4/MXFP8 with fused two-level block scaling (OCP MX spec) — it's what Blackwell, HC2, and the ecosystem converged on. Going below 4-bit (custom 3-bit, ternary/BitNet popcount-adder trees) buys ~25–90% bandwidth/area but bets the chip on quality/training regimes; Taalas's own retreat from 3-bit is the cautionary datapoint.
- **Determinism vs dynamism:** compiler-scheduled VLIW/stream designs (Groq, TPU lesson: *compiler* compatibility is the contract) eliminate caches/coherence — huge area/power wins — but demand a world-class compiler team from day one. MoE routing and speculative decoding inject dynamism; if your target class includes MoE, you need programmable routing and enough memory tiers for experts (SambaNova's SRAM+HBM+DDR triple is the prescient design here).
- **Scale-out:** decide single-die-per-model (≤8–20B at N6/N4 densities) vs pipeline parallelism over PCIe (Taalas's low-bandwidth trick at batch-1) vs deterministic fabric (Groq ISCA 2022) vs wafer scale. Note the WaferLLM finding: naïve ports waste the hardware; budget algorithm work.
- **Headroom:** TPUv4i's lesson — models grow ~1.5x/year. If your silicon lives 3 years, design for the model class of 2 years out, not today's.

### Stage 3 — Precision & quality gate

Quantize the target model *first*, in software, and hold the eval bar you'll ship with: per the quantization literature, sub-4-bit weight-only quantization of 8B-class models typically costs several MMLU points and disproportionately more on GSM8K/generation. Publish the delta. (No shipped specialist has; the first one to do so buys real credibility.)

### Stage 4 — RTL → verification → tapeout, at three ambition tiers

Verification dominates: designers spend 49% of their time on it, verifier:designer ratios run ~1:1, and **first-silicon success is down to 14% of projects** — budget a respin ([Siemens/Wilson 2024](https://verificationacademy.com/topics/planning-measurement-and-analysis/wrg-industry-data-and-trends/2024-siemens-eda-and-wilson-research-group-ic-asic-functional-verification-trend-report/)). EDA access has eased: Synopsys startup discounts (50–70% off under $10M revenue) and pay-per-minute cloud FlexEDA ([Synopsys](https://www.synopsys.com/blogs/chip-design/what-is-flexeda.html)). FPGA prototyping is cheap (AWS F2 at $3.96–15.84/hr; [AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/ec2/instance-types/f2/)).

**Tier 1 — Open-source / learning ($70–$15K, 1–5 people, 6–12 months to 130nm silicon).**
Tiny Tapeout tile (~€70, SkyWater/IHP/GF shuttles — [tinytapeout.com](https://tinytapeout.com/chips/)) → ChipFoundry chipIgnite full Caravel SoC ($14,950 incl. 100 packaged parts — [chipfoundry.io](https://chipfoundry.io/faqs)) → IHP SG13G2 free MPW for academics ([IHP open PDK](https://www.ihp-microelectronics.com/services/research-and-prototyping-service/fast-design-enablement/open-source-pdk)). Tools: OpenROAD RTL-to-GDSII (600+ tapeouts, proven to GF 12nm — [OpenROAD](https://theopenroadproject.org/openroad-key-milestones-on-the-road-towards-good-ppa/)); Chipyard + **Gemmini** systolic-array generator for a real (tiny) matmul accelerator ([GitHub](https://github.com/ucb-bar/gemmini)); a BitNet-ternary micro-inference engine is a genuinely publishable student project (cf. BitROM/TOM). *Deliverable: education and a die photo — nothing at this node touches competitive inference.*

**Tier 2 — Seed ($20–60M, 15–30 people, 2.5–4 years, mature/mid node, no HBM).**
Calibration points: Groq's first silicon on GF 14nm took roughly $60–70M raised and 3–4 years ([Chipstrat](https://www.chipstrat.com/p/the-rise-of-groq-slow-then-fast)); chip startups on TSMC 7nm report $50–75M all-in; a 12nm tapeout runs $3–5M; 28nm mask sets ~$1M, 7/5nm ~$5–15M (sources conflict on exact node pricing — [SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/the-dark-side-of-the-semiconductor), [Silicon Analysts](https://siliconanalysts.com/analysis/fabless-startup-tapeout-cost-guide)). Taalas's "24 people, $30M spent" is the tantalizing datapoint here — but it is **founder-reported and unaudited**, and hardwired chips skip most of the programmable-accelerator verification burden; do not anchor your budget on it. Architecture is forced: SRAM/ROM-resident weights or LPDDR/GDDR (HBM/CoWoS unobtainable). De-risk via MPW shuttles (28nm at ~$12–14K/mm² — [Muse/Europractice via vlsishuttle](https://www.vlsishuttle.com/en/learn/mpw-guide)) and FPGA prototypes; Positron's FPGA-first-revenue path ($230M Series B while shipping Altera-based appliances before its own tapeout — [Businesswire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260204250472/en/Positron-AI-Raises-$230-Million-Series-B-at-Over-$1-Billion-Valuation-to-Scale-Energy-Efficient-AI-Inference)) is the proven de-risking template. First-time tapeout budgets are "routinely underestimated 2–3x."

**Tier 3 — Funded ($500M–1B, 100+ people, 4–5 years, HBM + CoWoS at 4–6nm).**
Calibration: Etched ~$800M and ~4 years to first-pass A0 on N4P; MatX raised $500M explicitly "to get through tapeout," targeting 2027 shipping ([MatX](https://matx.com/research/series_b)); Microsoft Maia 200 slipped 6+ months even with hyperscaler resources. Cost drivers: 5nm-class design NRE $200–400M+ by full accounting; HBM3E 20–26-week leads; CoWoS 40–52-week leads with Nvidia consuming ~60% of allocation ([Silicon Analysts](https://siliconanalysts.com/analysis/cowos-packaging-cost-chiplet-vs-monolithic-2026)). Emulation (ZeBu/Palladium class) and a UVM army are non-negotiable at this tier.

### Stage 5 — The software/compiler stack

Budget a compiler/runtime team from day one, growing to roughly RTL-team size by ship. TVM/VTA are effectively dead post-OctoAI-acquisition; the live open paths are **MLIR-based (IREE, custom dialects, StableHLO ingestion)** or a fully custom compiler (the Groq/TPU road) ([Modular's post-mortem series](https://www.modular.com/blog/democratizing-ai-compute-part-6-what-about-ai-compilers)). Per TPUv4i, the compiler *is* your compatibility contract across silicon generations. A model-hardwired design inverts the problem: the "compiler" becomes a model-to-mask flow (graph → layout → two metal masks), which is Taalas's actual crown jewel — and the reason AMD bought a team, not just a die.

### Stage 6 — Go/no-go economics

**NRE amortization vs GPUs (worked arithmetic; assumptions flagged):**
- A mature-node, no-HBM program: ~$50–150M effective NRE for the platform; per-model marginal cost with a working structured-ASIC flow: ~$1–10M (two metal masks + requal) — vs ~$3M/variant in zach.be's independent estimate of a 30-variant DeepSeek-class deployment ([zach.be](https://www.zach.be/p/taalas-is-what-etched-should-have)).
- If one ~$5K no-HBM ASIC replaces ~5 GPUs' worth of *per-user* throughput (conservative vs vendor 48–73x claims), each unit avoids ~$150K of GPU capex, and $150M NRE breaks even near ~1,000 deployed chips — **but only if** the hardwired model stays in demand through a ~4–6-month build cycle plus payback. At 6–12-month model half-lives the window compresses brutally.
- Realistic go conditions (all must hold): (1) a weight-stable model family with ≥12 months of deployed life at ~10K+ chip-equivalents; (2) power-capped sites where perf/W converts directly to revenue per provisioned GW (the 2026 buildouts are denominated in GW — [OpenAI/Broadcom 10GW](https://openai.com/index/openai-and-broadcom-announce-strategic-collaboration/)); (3) a validated respin platform making per-model NRE ~$1–10M; (4) workloads that *price* single-user speed (agents, deep reasoning chains — cf. premium latency tiers at ~25x standard $/token); (5) ideally, a customer who controls its own model roadmap.
- **Headwinds you must beat:** $/token for fixed capability falls ~10x/year on general hardware ([a16z "LLMflation"](https://a16z.com/llmflation-llm-inference-cost/); Epoch measures 9x–900x/yr) — your frozen chip competes in 12 months against GPUs serving better models 10x cheaper; GPU MBU of 60–80% means Nvidia hardware is not as unbalanced as headline multiples imply ([wafer.substack](https://wafer.substack.com/p/breaking-down-etcheds-sohu)); and a retired model makes your inventory's salvage value ~zero, versus GPUs' liquid resale market.
- **Model-churn mitigations, ranked by evidence:** (a) structured-ASIC metal-only respins (Taalas — plausible, undemonstrated); (b) freeze the architecture, not the weights (Etched/MatX — HBM cost returns); (c) hardwire only frozen *components* (base weights in ROM + LoRA/KV in SRAM — shipped in HC1; BitROM academically); (d) target *stable* open-weight lineages whose fine-tunes share a base (Llama 3.1 family); (e) FPGA-first revenue while the target stabilizes (Positron — proven); (f) sell to a whale who controls its model roadmap (the entire hyperscaler-XPU market).

## 6. Honest Verdict

**When model-specific silicon wins.** Batch-1 interactive decode of a stable, high-volume, licensable model in power-capped facilities, sold on per-user latency and J/token. The physics is real: weights-in-ROM demonstrably delivers per-user token rates (14–17K tok/s) that no HBM machine reaches at any batch size, at ~0.015 J/token vs 1–30 for SRAM/GPU alternatives (vendor figures, order-of-magnitude corroborated). The 2026 conditions — inference at ~60–66% of AI compute, GW-denominated buildouts, HBM scarcity, open-weight model lineages stable enough to freeze — are the most favorable this bet has ever seen.

**When it loses.** Anywhere users insist on the newest model (coding is the canonical counterexample — the largest LLM application strongly prefers frontier models, as the sharpest skeptic notes: "if nobody is willing to use out-of-date LLMs, all those performance issues are for naught" — [zach.be](https://www.zach.be/p/taalas-is-what-etched-should-have)); long-context enterprise workloads (SRAM-bound KV caps context at thousands, not hundreds of thousands, of tokens); high-batch commodity serving where GPU amortization plus 10x/year software deflation outruns a frozen advantage; and any scenario where the two-month respin — the load-bearing, still-undemonstrated claim — fails to keep pace with architectural (not just weight) churn: MoE routing, hybrid SSM layers, attention variants.

**What the acquisition signals.** Three things, in descending confidence. First: **the industry's verdict on specialization economics is "portfolio, not replacement"** — AMD explicitly pairs Taalas silicon *alongside* Instinct GPUs, exactly as the codec precedent (harden the kernel inside a general platform) and the eASIC precedent (the intermediate point gets absorbed by platform owners) predicted. Second: **the memory wall, not FLOPS, is now priced as the binding constraint on inference** — AMD's whole 2024–2026 acquisition run (MEXT, Untether, MK1, Taalas) attacks memory cost from every angle. Third, and most tentative: with Groq's IP inside Nvidia, Taalas inside AMD, and reported Qualcomm–Tenstorrent talks, **the era of independent inference-ASIC companies is closing**; the remaining independents (Etched at $10.3B, MatX, Fractile) carry billion-dollar marks on zero independent benchmarks, and their exits will likely rhyme. The Taalas price, when disclosed at closing, will be the cleanest public read on what model-in-silicon capability is worth to an incumbent — until then, the only honest statement is that AMD paid an undisclosed amount for 24 engineers, ~14 patents, one demonstrated chip, and an undemonstrated respin flow.
